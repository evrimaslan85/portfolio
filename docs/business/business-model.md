# Commercial options and validation

All options below are hypotheses. No market-size, competitor superiority, willingness-to-pay, conversion, revenue, or pricing claim has been validated.

## A useful starting position

Build a complete free core around structured case studies and portable content. Use the existing website as an early installation once a tested migration is available. Learn whether people value the structure, evidence handling, and reduced publishing effort before expanding the product line.

| Offer | What a buyer receives | What needs validation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Free WordPress plugin | Working case studies, grid, basic styling, and public JSON portability | Can a new user publish a useful case study without support? |
| Standalone open-source kit | Local publisher, source content, and portable output | Is file-based authoring acceptable to the intended user? |
| Setup/migration service | Installation, design configuration, reviewed content migration, training | Which steps need human help, and what effort is repeatable? |
| Annual support/update plan | Defined response scope and update access for supported packages | Are users buying support, and can it be delivered sustainably? |
| Separate premium extension | Additional tested layouts, workflow, or integrations | Is there a distinct paid need without weakening the useful free product? |
| Hosted publishing service | Hosting, editor, backups, collaboration, custom-domain tools | Is recurring demand sufficient to fund operations and security? |

## Pricing research

Do not publish invented price tiers as if they are an offer. Interview prospective users about their current process and budget; test specific packages and service scopes; compare the cost of onboarding, updates, support, infrastructure, and payment administration. Separate personal-site support from agency-service scope without restricting open-source rights.

An initial commercial experiment could be a clearly scoped setup service rather than a subscription platform. It tests willingness to pay while revealing repeated usability issues. Service scope, actual price, delivery date, refunds, and support obligations are separate owner decisions.

## Optional community growth

Keep the basic download public and offer a separate update list for people who want release news and practical tips. The list is a relationship with people who choose it, not a registry of everyone using the software. Do not add downloaders, GitHub users, WordPress administrators, or their website visitors automatically. Use confirmed signup, clear purpose, and unsubscribe; see the [download and signup plan](downloads-and-signup.md). No email collection is connected today.

## Evidence to collect

1. Ask five to eight consultants/agency owners to bring one approved case study and explain how they publish today. This is a proposed research sample, not completed research.
2. Give them the preview and then a working prototype; observe where they struggle, which sections they keep, and whether they need WordPress or standalone authoring.
3. Record time to first acceptable publication, requests for help, missing fields, publishing concerns, and willingness to use it again. Avoid collecting client-sensitive content as research collateral.
4. Compare relevant alternatives using a dated feature matrix and actual public sources. No competitor research is asserted in this repository.
5. Offer a concrete pilot only after there is a working package and defined support scope. Track repeat use over several updates, not just first impressions.

## Success measures

Completion rate, time to first publication, edits without developer help, export/import fidelity, support time, accessibility defects, and repeat publishing are practical early signals. Conversion and revenue measures can follow if collected lawfully and with appropriate permission. The products have no default telemetry; research does not silently add tracking to customer sites.

## Costs and responsibilities

Budget for theme compatibility, WordPress/PHP updates, security response, documentation, user support, migrations, licenses/assets, and maintaining the portable contract. A hosted product adds backups, recovery, tenant isolation, account deletion, billing, outages, and processor/legal administration. No forecast can be credible until these costs and real demand are known.

## Decision gates

Ship the first WordPress alpha when it meets the acceptance checks; build the standalone alpha to prove portability; consider paid extensions after repeated specific requests; consider hosting after evidence that users need it and will fund its maintenance. Retire a form if it creates upkeep without a clear audience.
